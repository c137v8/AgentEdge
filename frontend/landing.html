Commerce breaks the moment you have to pay

Ask an AI shopping assistant to buy something today, and here's what actually happens: it recommends a product, then the conversation ends and a completely different app begins.

You leave the chat. You open your banking or UPI app. You find the right contact or scan a code. You re-enter an amount the AI already knew. You authenticate. You come back — if you come back — hoping the AI still remembers what you were doing.

Every one of those steps is a place the sale dies. The AI didn't fail at understanding what you wanted; it failed at the one moment that actually matters — finishing the transaction.

From recommending to acting

"Show me earbuds under 3000." "Add the mechanical keyboard." "Checkout." That's the entire interface — and unlike a typical shopping assistant, the last one doesn't just describe a checkout, it runs one.

Most AI agents are, structurally, recommendation engines wearing a chat UI: they can search and suggest, but the moment money needs to move, they stop and point you elsewhere. This project treats that as the actual problem to solve, not something to work around. The agent searches a real catalog, manages a live cart, and executes a real checkout-session lifecycle (built on the Agentic Commerce Protocol) — the same open standard OpenAI and Stripe use to let an agent, a buyer, and a merchant complete a purchase together.

The one thing it still won't do unilaterally: pull the trigger. Every checkout ends in an explicit "Confirm & Pay" card in the chat itself — everything up to that point is zero-click, but the human always makes the final call.

The plan: UPI Reserve Pay

The mechanism this is built around: block a certain amount of money once, and let the agent charge against that block for every purchase after — no repeated UPI prompts, no re-authentication per order.

That's the pattern behind Razorpay's own UPI Reserve Pay (Single Block, Multi Debit) — a user authorizes one block with a hard ceiling, and the merchant can then debit against it multiple times, up to that ceiling, with no further customer action required. It's exactly the primitive an autonomous shopping agent needs: authorization happens once, up front, under the user's full attention; execution happens many times, silently, within limits the user already set.

Honestly: SBMD isn't live in this build yet. Multi-debit activation requires Razorpay to enable it on a business account, which isn't self-serve. What is real today: the initial block is a genuine Razorpay test-mode order plus HMAC signature verification, and the repeated-debit lifecycle after that runs on a guardrail engine built to the same state machine (block → authorize → charge) so it's a drop-in swap once SBMD access is available — not a rewrite.

Secure by default, honest about the rest

No API keys checked in, no keys required to try it.

If the server has no .env keys, the app asks for them in the browser instead — held only in server memory for that session, never written to disk or logged. Skip the prompt entirely and the app drops into a fully local test mode: a rule-based agent, a simulated payment rail, and a persistent on-screen ribbon making it unmistakable that nothing being shown is a real charge.

The project is equally upfront about what's simulated elsewhere — see the README's "what's real vs. simulated" table. Nothing here is exaggerated to look further along than it is.
